## 2. The re-run vtable sweep: population 224 vs 76, and the named dtor family is CLEAN

L7 §9 asked for the sweep to be re-run "without the name-shaped regex", having
diagnosed that its regex required the expanded mangling tail so back-ref-spelled
instantiations were invisible.

**Two corrections to that diagnosis, from measuring it:**

* **Selecting map rows by PREFIX instead of by expanded tail changes nothing.**
  Rows matching `??1?$ObjRefConcrete@` in *any* spelling number **76** — the same
  76. Only 2 of them are non-expanded. So the under-selection was never about the
  spelling of the rows it examined.
* **The real defect is deeper: selecting by NAME AT ALL makes the population a
  function of the thing under test.** A dtor at an address the map calls something
  else, or calls nothing, cannot appear however the regex is written.

So this sweep selects by **structure**: every retail function, bounded by its own
`.pdata` extent, that installs a vtable whose `??_R4` COL resolves to a class
named `?$ObjPtr@...`. Instrument: `tools/retail_rtti.py` (imported, not
re-derived — its docstring records two lanes baking in wrong address arithmetic).
Selftest 8/8; the `naive-va` and `overscan` sabotage legs collapse it to 2/8 and
5/8, so the instrument discriminates.

| — of those, map T **AGREES** with retail RTTI | **71** |
| — of those, map T **DISAGREES** | **0** |

## 3. The two map errors, in the CTOR half (landed: `18cf2377`, +4 fns / +756 B)

Most of the 20 "named something else" rows are legitimate — a class ctor

rows whose *own name* is in the `ObjPtr<T>` ctor family gives 8, of which 5 agree,
1 is unresolved, and **2 disagree**:

| address | old name | new name | evidence | financed by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `0x8229ef00` | `??0?$ObjPtr@VRndMat@@@@QAA@ABV0@@Z` | `…VRndDir@@…` | RTTI vtable ⇒ `ObjPtr<RndDir>`; **our own source** already calls the RndDir spelling at both named callers; alias group 1119 independently found the same 17-address family | `LayerDir::Layer` copy ctor (224 B), `_Copy_Construct<SmasherPlateInfo>` (68 B), **and its own row** (120 B) |

3. **`symbol_aliases.json` group 1119**, whose members lane ALIASFIX had already

Each financing caller has **exactly one charged site**, a `diff_arg` on this name

## 4. The 132 unnamed: a 13× over-prediction, and the vein is a DUD (landed: `fdfb1cbf`, +1 fn / +120 B)

Calibrating kind by retail extent over the *named* members is sharp:
**ext=120 ⇒ copy ctor** (and group 1119 independently says all 17 such bodies are
120 B), **ext=116 ⇒ `~ObjRefConcrete<T>`**, ext=112 ⇒ 2-arg ctor. **ext=100 is
ambiguous** (24 named dtors vs 4 named ctors).

My structural sweep's 16 unnamed at ext=120, **plus `0x8229ef00`, are exactly
group 1119's 17-address census** — two completely different methods (COL/RTTI
resolution vs relocation-normalized body hashing) agreeing on the same set. That
is the strongest validation this sweep gets.

### ⛔ A detector that confirmed my prior, caught only by its control

To split the ambiguous ext=100 set I used `owning_vtables` — a dtor should appear
*in* the `ObjPtr<T>` vtable, a ctor should not. It returned **0 of 31 in any
vtable ⇒ all ctors**: unanimous, decisive-looking, and exactly what I expected.

**The control refutes it. Run on 12 KNOWN, map-named, RTTI-agreeing
`~ObjRefConcrete<T>` dtors, it finds 0/12.** The instrument cannot return a
positive on this population at all (`retail_rtti`'s own docstring records known
false negatives in this direction). The "0/31" was **vacuous**, and it would have
licensed 31 name assignments on no evidence.

The replacement discriminator **can** fail, because it returns two different
answers: retail dtors call `0x8275b378`, ctors call `0x8275bd08` — unanimous and
distinct across both calibration sets. En route this identifies **`0x8275bd08` =
`Object::AddRef` and `0x8275b378` = its `Release` counterpart**, both unnamed in
the map; they are what the earlier rows showed as `Target only: fn_8275BD08`
against our `?AddRef@Object@Hmx@@`.

### The measurement, and the honest reason it failed

9 of 16 passed the pairability gate. I named 8 and **pre-registered +7 fns /
+840 B**, reasoning from §3 where a correctly-named 120 B copy ctor crossed at
120/120.

**MEASURED: Δmatched +0, Δcode_bytes +64.** The accounting is exact:
CharMeshHide **+1 (+120 B)** and DataFile **−1 (−56 B)**; 120 − 56 = 64. Seven of
eight namings bought **nothing**.

Why, stated as the rule it is: **naming an anonymous address pays only through the
PAIRING channel, never the caller channel** — `name_check` already forgives a
placeholder target, so there is no caller-side upside to collect — **and pairing
pays only if the body then matches.** Six rows paired and still scored <100.
CLAUDE.md says this in advance; I priced it as if the RndDir precedent
generalised, and it does not. **13× over-prediction, the largest miss in the lane.**

DataFile's −56 B was diagnosed rather than reverted blind. `0x822b1728` has **11
`bl` sites across 6 callers with mutually inconsistent element types**:
`_Param_Construct<ObjPtr<EventTrigger>>` (template-forced, so the RTTI identity is
genuinely *right*), but **also** `vector<ObjPtr<RndPropAnim>>::_M_fill_insert_aux`
and `pair<const String,DataNode>`'s ctor. That is an **ICF survivor with real
folded twins**, so a bare name charges our *correct* spelling at every twin — the
"folded callee resolves to the survivor's single arbitrary name" case. The remedy
for a survivor is a T1-proven alias, not a name, and **I did not fabricate one.**
Withdrawn; re-measured at 7 rows ⇒ **+1 fn / +120 B, pre-registration EXACT,
0 regressions.**

**Also refused, for the record:** `0x8229dc70` (RndTransformable) — 23 retail
callers, **19 of them rows currently at fuzzy 100**. The ceiling is one row's
120 B; the exposure is 19 rows. Not verifiable within budget. 5 more refused
because the pinned unit's obj does not define the name (permanent 0% by
construction), 2 have no unit pin, and `0x82304720` (GemTrackDir) has a spelling
our objs never emit.

## 6. L5 handoff 1 DISSOLVES: `0x823a4fa8` is not a function

L5 left `ObjPtrList<RndMesh>::Link` @`0x8227d020` (196 B) vs
`<CharCollide>::Link` @`0x823a4fa8` (260 B) as "not a fold, so one name is wrong",
and proposed settling it by callee set as its §3 did. Both halves come apart:

* **The callee channel cannot settle it.** `0x8227d020`'s only semantic callee is
  `Object::AddRef`; its other `bl` goes to `0x8282925c`, which is **+60 inside an
  80-byte function** — the MSVC `__savegprlr_*` prologue-helper family, entered
  mid-body by design, not a callee. So the body carries **no per-T datum**,
  exactly as L7 §10 predicted for this row.
* **`0x823a4fa8` is not a function start.** It is not a `.pdata` BeginAddress and
  lies in a **264-byte `.pdata`-uncovered gap** (the preceding extent ends exactly
  at it, the next begins at `0x823a50b0`). Its first words are
  `lfs f13,0x200(r3)` / `lfs f0,8(r4)` / `fsubs f0,f0,f13` — **floating-point math
  with no prologue**, which an `ObjPtrList<T>::Link` categorically is not.

⇒ The question "which of the two names is wrong" has an answer L5 did not
anticipate: **`0x823a4fa8`'s is, and not because of T — because there is nothing
there to name.** This is the `★★★★★ ASK IF THE THING NEEDING A NAME EXISTS`
hazard: a phantom row from a bad carve is indistinguishable from an unidentified
one until you check byte geometry, and L5's "260 B" came from the carve, not from
`.pdata`. `0x8227d020`'s T remains unsettled.

I did **not** null `0x823a4fa8` — that is a measurement of its own and it belongs
with the splits question of why a 264-byte gap is being carved as a symbol.
